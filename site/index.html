How it works

One registration. A signal platforms can honour. A record they can’t game.

SafeGen doesn’t watch your child, scan their face, or sit on their phone. It lets a platform recognise a number a parent has vouched for — and it gives you the tools to see whether they’re listening.

1

You vouch, once

A FaceIQ check confirms an adult is present, on your own device. You enter your child’s mobile number and its status. A code sent to their phone proves the number is really theirs.

Best done together — many families pair it with the “you can have the phone, not the apps” conversation. No bank ID, no documents, no birthday.

2

The number becomes a code

Before anything is stored, the number is transformed using keys split between two independent organisations. Neither can reverse it alone. The real number is destroyed in seconds.

This isn’t a pinky-promise deletion — the system has nowhere to store a phone number — and independent auditors verify that before launch, with the reports published.

3

Platforms recognise, privately

When someone signs up (or already has an account) with that number, a participating platform’s own systems get one quiet signal: a parent has vouched this number belongs to a minor.

The platform then runs its strictest age check. If a flag is ever wrong — say, a recycled number — an adult passes that check once. A wrong flag costs one extra check, not a lockout.

You can act today

Test the platforms yourself — in about five minutes.

You don’t have to wait for platforms to cooperate to find out where your child’s number already lives. As their parent, holding their phone, you can check — and SafeGen guides you and records what you find.

1

You hold the phone, together

The test only works on a number you physically hold, with your child beside you. You’re their guardian, you consent, and the phone is in your hand — this is a parent checking on their own child, nothing more.

2

SafeGen guides the check

Step by step, SafeGen walks you through asking each platform whether that number is already tied to an account — using the platform’s own “is this number registered?” and code-to-your-phone flows, which arrive on the phone you’re holding.

3

The result becomes evidence

Each answer is recorded the same way: this platform does — or doesn’t — have an account tied to a known child’s number, on this date. Collected consistently, these are exactly the records the Commissioner’s office can act on.

⚖️

Why this is documented parental discovery — not covert probing

Three things make it legitimate, and they’re the same three every time: you are the child’s guardian, you give consent, and you are holding the phone that receives whatever a platform sends back. SafeGen never touches an account and never triggers anything on a number you don’t hold.

It’s a standing SafeGen rule: detection runs only over authorised, consented pathways — never covert scraping or circumvention. A parent testing their own child’s number, on their own child’s phone, is exactly that.

Don’t want to do it alone? Trained volunteers can walk you through it, and short how-to videos show the whole process end to end — so every parent can take part, and every test is collected the same careful way.
Nothing hidden

What we hold. What we never hold.

This is the entire record SafeGen keeps about your family. Not a summary — the whole thing. And it holds only children: the moment a child becomes an adult, the record is deleted, never converted.

✓  What we hold (all of it)

  • ✓A scrambled code made from your child’s number32 random-looking characters. Turning it back into a phone number would require breaking into two separate organisations’ security hardware at once.
  • ✓A status flag: Child or Child-with-consentJust enough to tell platforms how to treat the number. No age, no expiry date, no birthday, nothing derived from one.
  • ✓A fingerprint of your undo keySo you can remove it any time — without us knowing who you are.

✕  What we never hold

  • ✕Your child’s name, birthday, age, photo, school — or their actual phone number
  • ✕Your name, ID documents, or contact detailsYour FaceIQ check happens on your device and is used for a moment, then gone. We couldn’t contact you if we wanted to — so keep your undo key safe.
  • ✕Any record of which apps checked, or what they foundWhen a platform matches, it happens inside the platform. We never see it.
  • ✕Any adult, everWhen you flag the child as an adult, or they move to a new number, the record is deleted — not kept, not archived, not “converted”.
Why the law is on your side here: Australia’s Online Safety Act (section 63F) requires that personal information collected for age checks be destroyed after use — and the Privacy Commissioner (OAIC) enforces it. SafeGen was designed backwards from that rule: the only thing that survives your visit is a code that identifies no one, carrying a status flag and kept with your explicit consent, for only as long as it’s a child.

Safe by design

“Is this a government database of children?” No. Here’s why it can’t become one.

We didn’t write a privacy policy and hope. We built a system where the scary version can’t be built quietly — breaking the promise would require two independent organisations acting together, in public view.

🧩

The keys are split

Unscrambling even one code needs two separate organisations — SafeGen and an independent trustee — to act together. A hacker, a rogue employee, or a demand served on either one gets random bytes.

Double-blind checks

Platforms check numbers in scrambled form, and the match happens on their side. We never learn which child is on which app. They never get a list — only answers about numbers they already have.

🚫

Authorised pathways only

SafeGen never uses covert scraping or circumvention. Every check — including the parent-run test — runs only over consented, authorised pathways. It’s a hard rule, not a preference.

🗑️

Children only, then gone

The list holds only children. Flag your child as an adult, or move them to a new number, and the record is deleted — not converted, not archived. And your undo key removes it any time, within 24 hours.

📊

Watched in public

Every system update and every platform’s query volume goes into a public record that no one can quietly edit — so unusual behaviour is visible to researchers and regulators, not just to us.

🧯

A real off switch

If SafeGen ever had to shut down, destroying the two keys means no stored code — including any old backup, anywhere — can ever be matched or checked again.

Questions parents ask

Fair questions. Straight answers.

What actually happens the day I register — before platforms have joined? +

Two useful things at once. Your number joins a body of parent-verified registrations that gives the eSafety Commissioner real evidence — and you can immediately run the guided test to see which platforms already have your child’s number tied to an account. If a platform later honours the signal, your child is protected there; if it doesn’t, your test has helped prove it. You’re never just waiting.

Is the “test the platforms” part legal? It sounds like probing. +

It’s a parent checking on their own child — the oldest legitimate thing there is. You are the child’s guardian, you give consent, and you are physically holding the phone that receives anything a platform sends back. SafeGen only guides you and records the result; it never touches an account and never acts on a number you don’t hold. That’s documented parental discovery, not covert probing — and SafeGen has a hard rule against covert scraping or circumvention of any kind.

Can platforms see my child’s phone number or identity? +

They never receive anything from us. Platforms already have the phone numbers of their own users — that’s how signup works. SafeGen only lets a platform check, in scrambled form, whether a number it already holds was vouched for by a parent. It learns yes or no about numbers it already has — it cannot browse, search, or download a list.

You said no birthday — so how does the “under 16” part work? +

We don’t store an age or a birthday at all. We store a status: Child for an under-16, or Child-with-consent where you’re flagging a 16- or 17-year-old’s number on their behalf. When your child becomes an adult, you flag it — or they move to a new number — and the record is deleted, not converted. Nothing on the list is ever an adult.

Do you check my ID or my bank? +

No. Adult confirmation is a FaceIQ check that runs on your own device — it confirms a real adult is present without documents, bank checks, or anything leaving your phone. If you’re ever wrongly caught on the list as an adult, you can clear yourself the same way: a quick on-device FaceIQ check (with an optional licence-photo comparison for borderline ages), where our servers only ever see a pass/fail — never your face or your documents.

Can I undo this? +

Yes, any time. At registration you get an undo key — a short code we show you once. Enter it on this site and the record is removed within 24 hours. Lost the key? Removal is still possible with a confirmation code to the phone plus a fresh FaceIQ adult check — so a parent can always undo it, but a child can’t quietly unenrol themselves.

Is this a government database? Will eSafety or police get the list? +

No — and it’s the part we’re proudest of. There is no list. What we store is scrambled codes that identify no one, reversible only if two independent organisations’ secure hardware were compromised at once. A subpoena served on SafeGen yields random bytes. The Commissioner receives evidence and statistics parents choose to contribute; they audit how we operate, and never get a feed of children — nothing exists that could be fed.

Is there really a “wall of shame”? Isn’t that just naming and blaming? +

There’s a record of proven non-compliance, yes — but it’s built to be fair, not vengeful. It stays private until there’s a real body of proven cases; a platform is only named against a defined evidence standard and after a right of reply; and it’s aimed at the Commissioner and parents as evidence, not at headlines. Most importantly, there’s an equal-and-opposite honour roll: the first platform to integrate is celebrated as the one that chose to protect kids, and any platform moves across the moment it does the right thing. Firm where there’s harm, generous the instant there’s good faith.

Which apps does this work on? +

Participating platforms — the goal is every service covered by Australia’s under-16 law, and the pilot starts with the biggest ones. We publish the live list right here, and it should grow: honouring the signal counts toward the “reasonable steps” the law already demands. On an app that doesn’t participate yet, the flag does nothing — but your guided test still documents whether that app has your child’s number, which is exactly the evidence that moves them.

It’s free for parents — so who pays? +

Platforms pay — 1¢ AUD per check, the same way they already pay for fraud and security checks, and cheap next to the fines for getting it wrong. Parents never pay a cent. And your family’s data can’t quietly become the product, for a simple reason: we don’t hold any to sell.

Can’t my child just get a second SIM, or a number I don’t know about? +

Honestly — yes. A determined teenager with a secret prepaid SIM is outside what SafeGen can see. It covers the number you know about, which for most families is the number the child actually lives on. What it removes are the easy workarounds — new account, new app, new birthday, one more selfie attempt. Think seatbelt, not cage.

Why should I trust you? Every company says they care about privacy. +

Don’t trust us — check us. Our protocol is published for anyone to review; certified auditors verify before launch that the system physically has no place to keep phone numbers; every software version and every platform’s query volume goes into a public record no one can quietly edit; and the OAIC can hold us to section 63F, which makes keeping your data illegal, not just off-brand. We designed SafeGen so that trusting us is never the load-bearing part.

Is this compulsory? Whose idea is it? +

Entirely voluntary, and it exists because parents kept asking for something that actually works. Australia’s under-16 law made platforms responsible for keeping kids off — but their checks can be gamed and reset. SafeGen gives your decision, as the parent, a way to stick and a way to prove it: across accounts, across apps.
